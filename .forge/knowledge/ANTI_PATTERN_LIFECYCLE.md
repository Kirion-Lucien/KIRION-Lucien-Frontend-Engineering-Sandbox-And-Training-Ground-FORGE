# ANTI-PATTERN LIFECYCLE

## Purpose

Lucien models harmful recurring frontend structures with evidence. It does not implement the subjective rule “looks AI-generated = bad.”

An anti-pattern claim is valid only when the recurring structure, harmful mechanism, applicable context, legitimate exceptions, consequences, supporting evidence, counterexamples, and uncertainty are reviewable.

## Required evidence model

An anti-pattern candidate must describe:

- observed recurring structure;
- why and how it becomes harmful;
- contexts where the criticism applies;
- contexts where the same structure may be legitimate;
- usability consequences;
- accessibility consequences;
- performance consequences;
- maintainability consequences;
- supporting source IDs and observation IDs;
- counterexamples;
- confidence;
- recency/version constraints;
- acceptance state.

## Lifecycle

OBSERVED
→ CROSS-SOURCE COMPARISON
→ CANDIDATE
→ MAINTAINER REVIEW
→ ACCEPTED / REJECTED
→ DEPRECATED when superseded

An anti-pattern is not accepted because it is aesthetically disliked, frequently mocked, associated with generated interfaces, or repeated across social media.

## Challenge law

Every candidate must be challengeable. A legitimate counterexample can narrow scope, lower confidence, trigger rework, support rejection, or later deprecate accepted knowledge.

The question is not “does this look like AI?” but “what observable structure causes what documented harm under what context, and what evidence and counterexamples bound that claim?”
